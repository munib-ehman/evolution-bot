Supplier emails get answered faster when the recipient can see the order, decision, deadline, and required response without reconstructing a long thread. Put the purchase-order number and milestone in the subject, ask numbered questions, and request confirmation in the same numbered format.

Email should move a decision forward. The purchase order, approved specification, sample record, inspection criteria, and shipping documents remain the source of truth.

## Use one operating format

Start every material supplier message with:

- **Subject:** PO number + product + milestone + response date
- **Context:** one or two sentences about what changed or what is due
- **Facts:** quantities, version, dates, and attachments
- **Questions:** numbered decisions the supplier must answer
- **Next step:** who does what after confirmation
- **Deadline:** exact date, time, and time zone

Use dates such as `15 September 2026`, not `9/15`, and name the time zone. Refer to attachments by filename and version. If the supplier uses another first language, keep sentences short, avoid idioms, and ask them to restate critical numbers rather than replying only “confirmed.”

Shopify's current [purchase-order documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/inventory/purchase-orders) describes a PO as the record of products, quantities, costs, payment terms, and supplier details. Marking a PO as ordered follows supplier confirmation. That is a useful boundary: discuss by email, then update the commercial record once both sides agree.

## Template 1: Clarify a quote before approval

**Subject:** `Quote check — [product] — [quantity] — reply by [date]`

> Hi [name],
>
> Thank you for quote [quote number/version]. Before we approve it, please reply below each numbered item.
>
> 1. Confirm unit price for [quantity] using specification [filename/version].
> 2. Confirm whether the price includes [packaging, labels, tooling, testing].
> 3. Confirm sample cost and sample completion date.
> 4. Confirm production lead time starts from [deposit / final artwork / sample approval].
> 5. Confirm payment schedule and beneficiary legal name.
> 6. Confirm the proposed Incoterms® rule, named place, and version.
>
> Please return the completed answers and revised quote by [date, time, time zone]. We will issue or revise the purchase order after review.

Do not write only “FOB” or “DDP.” The International Chamber of Commerce's official [Incoterms® guidance](https://library.iccwbo.org/clp/clp-incoterms-qa-2020.htm) explains that the rules allocate specified obligations, risks, and costs between seller and buyer. Use the chosen rule with a named place and the current contractual version, and get qualified shipping or legal advice when responsibilities are unclear.

Before approving the quote, complete the broader [supplier sourcing checklist](/blog/sourcing-products-from-suppliers-checklist), including business verification, compliance, landed cost, and payment controls.

## Template 2: Approve a sample with measurable conditions

**Subject:** `Sample decision — PO [number] — [product/SKU] — [approved or revisions]`

> Hi [name],
>
> We reviewed sample [sample ID] received on [date] against specification [version].
>
> **Approved items**
> - [material/color/dimension/finish]
> - [packaging/label/barcode]
>
> **Required changes before production**
> 1. [current measurement] must change to [required measurement and tolerance].
> 2. [current component] must match [reference or approved file].
>
> Please send by [date]:
> 1. a revised pre-production photo or sample showing each change
> 2. your updated production start and completion dates
> 3. written confirmation that bulk production will match specification [version] and approved sample [ID]
>
> Production is not approved until we confirm the revised evidence in writing.

Avoid “looks good except the color.” Attach a dated image, measurement table, or marked file. If the change affects safety, labeling, or compliance, do not approve production based on a photo alone.

## Template 3: Confirm the production plan

**Subject:** `Production confirmation — PO [number] — reply by [date]`

> Hi [name],
>
> Before production starts, please confirm the current plan below.
>
> 1. Final specification: [filename/version]
> 2. Approved sample: [ID/date]
> 3. Quantity by SKU: [attach filename]
> 4. Materials ready date: [date]
> 5. Production start: [date]
> 6. Production complete: [date]
> 7. Inspection-ready date: [date]
> 8. Planned handoff or ship date: [date]
> 9. Named owner for production updates: [name/contact]
>
> Please identify any risk to these dates now and reply with the confirmed schedule by [date, time, time zone]. We will use this schedule for weekly checkpoints.

Separate the commercial agreement from physical movement. Shopify's current [linked transfer documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/inventory/purchase-orders/creating-inventory-transfers) keeps the PO as the agreement while the transfer tracks shipments, receiving, accepted or rejected quantities, and cost adjustments. That distinction prevents “ordered” from being confused with “shipped” or “available.”

## Template 4: Ask for a useful production update

**Subject:** `Weekly status — PO [number] — milestone [name] — reply by [date]`

> Hi [name],
>
> Please update each line using `complete`, `on track`, `at risk`, or `late`.
>
> 1. Materials: [status, quantity, evidence]
> 2. Production: [completed quantity / total quantity]
> 3. Quality checks: [completed checks and result]
> 4. Packaging: [completed quantity / total quantity]
> 5. Inspection-ready date: [date]
> 6. Handoff or ship date: [date]
>
> For any `at risk` or `late` item, include the cause, affected SKUs and quantities, recovery action, owner, and revised date. Please attach [specific photos/report] and reply by [date, time, time zone].

“Everything is okay” is not a status report. Ask for completed quantities and evidence tied to the next milestone. Keep the cadence predictable so the supplier can prepare the update instead of being chased at random.

## Template 5: Respond to a delay without accepting a vague date

**Subject:** `Action required — PO [number] delay — recovery plan by [date]`

> Hi [name],
>
> Your update changes [milestone] from [agreed date] to [proposed date]. This affects [launch, stock coverage, inspection, or shipping plan].
>
> Please confirm by [date, time, time zone]:
> 1. root cause of the delay
> 2. affected SKUs and quantities
> 3. work already complete
> 4. earliest realistic inspection-ready and handoff dates
> 5. recovery options, including split shipment or priority SKUs
> 6. additional cost and who would bear it
> 7. next evidence checkpoint
>
> We have not accepted the revised schedule or added cost. We will respond after reviewing the complete recovery plan against PO [number] and our agreement.

Do not threaten a chargeback or cancellation you are not prepared and entitled to execute. Preserve the written record, check the contract, and escalate material legal or payment questions to qualified counsel.

Connect the revised date to your [inventory forecast](/blog/forecast-demand-small-ecommerce-store). A split shipment may protect a core SKU while leaving a slow variant for later; it may also increase freight and customs costs. Compare the operational benefit with the full landed-cost change.

## Template 6: Report a quality problem

**Subject:** `Quality hold — PO [number] — [SKU] — response required [date]`

> Hi [name],
>
> Inspection or receiving identified the issue below against specification [version] and criterion [reference].
>
> - Affected SKU and quantity: [details]
> - Sample or lot: [details]
> - Observed result: [measurement/fact]
> - Required result: [measurement/tolerance]
> - Evidence: [photo/report filenames]
>
> Please confirm by [date]:
> 1. containment of affected units
> 2. root-cause investigation owner
> 3. proposed rework, replacement, or other remedy
> 4. evidence required before reinspection
> 5. revised completion and shipping dates
>
> Do not ship the affected units until we approve the disposition in writing.

Describe evidence, not blame. “Poor quality” is hard to act on; “17 of 80 inspected units measured 2–4 mm outside tolerance X” gives both sides something to resolve. For regulated consumer products, compliance and reporting duties can extend beyond the supplier relationship. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's [business reporting guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Recall-Guidance/Duty-to-Report-to-CPSC-Rights-and-Responsibilities-of-Businesses) explains that manufacturers, importers, distributors, and retailers can have reporting obligations. Get product-specific advice when safety may be involved.

## Template 7: Confirm shipping documents and handoff

**Subject:** `Shipping documents — PO [number] — required before [date]`

> Hi [name],
>
> Please send the final document set for PO [number] before cargo handoff:
>
> 1. commercial invoice
> 2. packing list with carton count, weights, dimensions, and SKU quantities
> 3. inspection approval or agreed release record
> 4. transport booking and handoff details
> 5. tracking, bill, or airway-bill reference when issued
> 6. required origin, testing, or compliance documents listed in the PO
>
> Confirm the shipper, consignee, named place, quantities, values, product descriptions, and Incoterms® rule match the approved records. Report any difference before handoff. Please send the draft set by [date] and final set by [date].

After handoff, record the shipment against incoming inventory and update the expected date. Do not promise customers an arrival based only on the factory's ship date. The [holiday cutoff guide](/blog/holiday-shipping-cutoffs-ecommerce) shows how fulfillment, transit, and risk buffers belong in customer-facing dates.

## Keep a decision log outside the inbox

For each PO, maintain a small table:

| Date | Decision or change | Evidence | Owner | Next deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-01 | Carton artwork v3 approved | signed PDF | buyer | pre-production photo 2026-09-04 |

After a call or chat, email a same-day summary: what was decided, what was not decided, changed cost or dates, responsible person, and response deadline. Ask the supplier to correct the record if it is wrong. Then revise the PO or specification when the commercial terms changed; do not leave the controlling decision buried in a meeting recap.

## Escalate predictably

Set escalation rules before a problem:

- first missed update: resend with the exact unanswered items
- second missed update: copy the named account or production manager
- milestone at risk: request a recovery plan and evidence call
- payment, compliance, safety, or material contract issue: stop the affected approval and involve the appropriate expert

The aim is not to write harsher email. It is to make every request answerable, every decision traceable, and every delay visible while options still exist.

Choose your highest-value open purchase order today. Replace its next vague “any update?” message with the milestone template, numbered questions, evidence request, and an exact response deadline.
