A meaningful chunk of shopping now starts with a question typed into ChatGPT instead of Google: "best ceramic travel mug that doesn't leak," "good linen sheets under $150." By 2026, roughly a third of product discovery queries start in an AI interface. When the answer comes back with three product recommendations, you're either one of them or you're invisible.

The practice of earning those recommendations is called AEO (answer engine optimization), sometimes GEO. Most of what's written about it targets enterprise brands with agencies. Here's what actually matters for a small Shopify store.

## The uncomfortable starting fact

Ranking well on Google does not mean AI tools recommend you. Top-10 Google results show up in AI answers only about 8% of the time. SEO rewards keywords, backlinks, and domain authority. AI answers reward something different: parseable product data, answer-shaped content, and what *other people* say about you.

That last one is the big one. LLMs pull 80%+ of their product recommendations from third-party sources (reviews, buying guides, Reddit threads, press), not from your own website. You can't copywrite your way into a ChatGPT answer. You earn your way in from the outside, and you make your store easy to verify from the inside.

Also worth knowing: ad spend has zero influence here. For once, small stores and big brands play on the same field.

## How AI decides who to recommend

Four signals, in rough order of weight:

1. **Third-party authority.** Review volume and sentiment, mentions in buying guides, community threads, niche press. This is the raw material AI answers are built from.
2. **Structured product data.** Complete Product schema (price, availability, ratings, GTIN). AI systems verify claims against structured data before repeating them.
3. **Answer-shaped content.** Pages that directly answer the questions shoppers ask, in the first paragraph, not after 800 words of throat-clearing.
4. **Consistency.** Same brand name, same product names, same claims everywhere. Agents cross-reference, and contradictions cost you trust scores.

## The checklist for a small Shopify store

### 1. Make sure AI can read your store at all

AI crawlers mostly don't run JavaScript. Standard Shopify themes render server-side and are fine, but check two things: your robots.txt isn't blocking GPTBot, PerplexityBot, or ClaudeBot, and any content added by third-party apps (reviews especially) exists in the page HTML, not just injected by a script. View source on a product page and search for your review text. If it's not there, AI can't see your social proof.

### 2. Get your Product schema complete

Shopify themes emit basic schema, but "basic" usually means missing GTINs, review ratings, or accurate availability. Every product page should carry Product schema with price, currency, availability, brand, and aggregate rating. This is a minimum requirement for being cited, not a bonus.

### 3. Write one real buying guide per category

Buying-guide content gets cited by AI at much higher rates than product pages. "How to choose a weighted blanket (and who shouldn't buy one)" earns citations; "Our Deluxe Weighted Blanket™" doesn't. Be genuinely useful, name honest tradeoffs, and include your own product where it truly fits. One thorough guide per category beats ten thin ones.

### 4. Feed the third-party layer

Since most recommendations come from outside your site:

- **Reviews first.** Volume matters. A steady post-purchase review ask is the single highest-leverage AEO action a small store can take, and [you don't need a paid reviews app](/blog/how-to-get-more-product-reviews) to run one.
- **Show up where your category is discussed.** Reddit, niche forums, community blogs. Helpfully, not promotionally. One genuine answer from a founder outlasts fifty dropped links.
- **Pitch inclusion in existing "best X" roundups.** Writers updating those lists for 2026 need candidates. A short pitch with real differentiators works more often than you'd think.

### 5. Answer questions in the first paragraph

Take your ten most common presale questions and make sure each has a page (or FAQ entry) that answers it in the first two sentences. AI systems extract answers; they don't summarize your brand story. This also happens to win you the presale conversations that AI didn't intercept. The same questions, answered fast, are [what AI support agents get built on](/blog/ai-agents-for-ecommerce-small-stores).

## What to skip

AEO tracking platforms and "AI visibility" dashboards are built for brands with teams. At your size, the free check is enough: ask ChatGPT and Perplexity the three questions a customer would ask before finding you ("best [your product] for [use case]"), note who gets recommended, and look at *why*: their review counts, the guides that mention them. That's your gap list. Re-run it monthly.

None of this is exotic. Clean data, honest answer-first content, and real reviews were already the right moves for converting human customers. AI search just raised the price of skipping them.
