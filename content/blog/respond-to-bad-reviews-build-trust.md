A useful response to a bad review does three things: it acknowledges the customer's experience, states what the store can verify or do next, and shows future shoppers how problems are handled. It does not argue the customer into changing a rating.

Treat the public reply as the start of a support process. Resolve order-specific details privately, then fix the underlying product, policy, or fulfillment issue if the review exposes a pattern.

## Classify the review before replying

Put the review into one of four buckets:

| Type | Example | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Specific and plausible | Wrong size, late delivery, damaged item | Verify facts, reply, resolve |
| Vague but relevant | “Disappointed” with no detail | Acknowledge and invite specifics |
| Factually disputed but policy-compliant | Customer and store disagree about timing or policy | Reply with neutral verified facts |
| Policy-violating | Spam, harassment, personal information, unrelated content | Preserve evidence and report |

Do not report a review merely because it is negative. Google says in its current [review reporting guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773) that only policy violations are eligible for removal and that disagreement is not a removal reason. Shopify similarly provides specific reporting categories for Shop reviews, including spam, harassment, personal information, conflicts of interest, and unrelated content in its [Shop review management guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shop/product-reviews/manage).

Take a screenshot and save the URL, date, platform, product, rating, and any order reference you can verify. Do not copy the customer's private order details into the public record.

## Use a five-part public response

Write the response from these parts:

1. **Recognition:** name the issue without exaggerating or minimizing it.
2. **Accountability:** own a verified store error, or state that you are investigating.
3. **Action:** say what can happen next.
4. **Private path:** give one channel for order-specific details.
5. **Closure:** set a truthful follow-up condition or timing.

A compact structure is usually enough:

> Hi [name] — I am sorry [specific issue] happened. We are checking [relevant fact or process]. Please contact us at [support channel] with [minimum identifying detail], and we will [verified next action] by [date or condition]. We will also review [upstream issue] so the same problem is less likely to repeat.

Do not paste a generic paragraph under every low rating. Repeated wording signals that nobody investigated the review. Use the same decision framework, but personalize the facts.

## Protect customer privacy in public

Never publish an order number, address, phone number, tracking detail, payment method, medical information, or full email address in a reply. If the customer included sensitive information, capture what you need for the support record and use the platform's reporting process where appropriate.

Move the conversation private with the minimum request necessary to find the order:

> Please email [support address] from the address used at checkout and mention this review, so we can locate the order without asking you to post personal details here.

Do not ask the customer to repeat evidence already present in an existing ticket. Link the review to the ticket internally, assign an owner, and keep one source of truth for the resolution.

## Response templates for common bad reviews

### The product did not match expectations

> Thank you for explaining what felt different from the listing. I am sorry [specific expectation] did not match what arrived. We are reviewing the product page and the details you mentioned. Please contact [channel] with your order email so we can confirm the return, exchange, or other option available under our policy.

Do not promise a refund publicly until eligibility and the actual action are confirmed. If the review reveals unclear sizing, color, materials, or compatibility, update the listing rather than treating the reply as the final fix. Use the [product-page mistakes checklist](/blog/product-page-mistakes-that-kill-conversions) to examine the page that set the expectation.

### The shipment was late

> I am sorry the delivery missed the expectation we gave you. I do not want to guess about the package status here. Please contact [channel] with the email used for the order, and we will verify the latest carrier event and update you by [date or time zone]. We are also reviewing where our delivery estimate was shown.

Avoid blaming the carrier before checking when the order was fulfilled, what estimate the store displayed, and whether proactive updates were sent.

### The customer says support never answered

> You should not have needed to follow up for a response. We are checking the inbox and order history to understand what was missed. Please contact [single channel] with [minimum detail]; [owner or team] will reply by [date]. We are also reviewing the routing rule that should have assigned the original message.

Do not tell the reviewer to open another ticket across several channels. One clear owner reduces duplicate work and conflicting answers.

### You cannot match the reviewer to an order

> We have not yet been able to match this review to an order using the public information available. We still want to investigate. Please contact [channel] from the checkout email and mention this review. We will verify the purchase and respond based on the order record.

Do not accuse the reviewer of lying. A different name, gift order, marketplace purchase, or old email address can explain the mismatch.

## Know when to report instead of debate

Report a review when it appears to violate the platform's published policy, such as exposed personal data, spam, threats, harassment, an undisclosed conflict of interest, or content unrelated to the transaction. Submit evidence and use the platform's stated category.

If the review remains published after review, do not repeatedly flag it under different reasons. Add a calm factual response if appropriate and continue the support process. Platform moderation is separate from whether your store can resolve the customer's issue.

Shopify allows merchants to reply to Shop product reviews from the admin and says the reply can be screened against its merchant guidelines. Those [Shop review guidelines](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shop/product-reviews/merchant-guidelines) require neutral review solicitation and prohibit fake reviews, conflicts of interest, and compensation for reviews or for changing or removing a review.

## Do not buy a cleaner rating

The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect on October 21, 2024. The FTC's current [rule questions and answers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) explain that a business cannot condition compensation or incentives on a review expressing a particular sentiment. The guidance also warns that paying someone to change or remove a truthful negative review may be deceptive even when that conduct is not covered by the rule's specific incentive provision.

Do not offer a refund, gift card, discount, or replacement on the condition that the customer edits or deletes the review. You may still provide the remedy your policy and the facts support. Keep that remedy separate from the customer's choice about the review.

Likewise, do not ask only satisfied customers for public reviews while routing unhappy customers somewhere hidden. Ask neutrally. The [product review collection guide](/blog/how-to-get-more-product-reviews) covers timing and a simple request process.

## Close the loop internally

After resolving the individual case, tag the root cause:

- product quality or packaging
- misleading photo, description, sizing, or compatibility information
- fulfillment or carrier delay
- missing proactive communication
- confusing return or refund policy
- support routing or response failure
- expectation the store cannot reasonably meet

Review tags monthly by product and process. A single unusual complaint may require only a fair resolution. Repeated complaints about the same SKU or promise require an upstream change.

For returns-related reviews, compare the complaint with the process in the [returns guide](/blog/returns-without-losing-the-customer). A polite public reply cannot repair a return flow that remains slow or unclear.

## Review the response before posting

Check:

- Is every factual statement verified?
- Did we expose any customer information?
- Did we promise only actions we can complete?
- Is there one private contact path and one owner?
- Does the tone stay calm if read without the original review?
- Are we fixing the underlying issue, not just the wording?

Respond when you have enough facts to be useful. If investigation will take time, acknowledge the review and state the next update. Future shoppers do not expect a store to prevent every problem; they can see whether the store takes ownership when one occurs.
