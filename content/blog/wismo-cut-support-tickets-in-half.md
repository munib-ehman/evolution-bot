If you've ever opened your support inbox on a Monday and seen twenty variations of "where's my order," you already know the feeling. It's not a hard question to answer, but answering it twenty times a day, every day, quietly eats the hours you meant to spend on anything else.

WISMO tickets (where is my order) are usually the single largest category in a small store's support queue. They're also the most fixable, because the answer already exists somewhere in your systems. The customer just can't see it.

## Why WISMO tickets pile up in the first place

Most stores lose the WISMO battle for the same three reasons:

- **Tracking info is buried.** The shipping confirmation email gets sent once, gets missed or deleted, and the customer has no easy way to check status again without emailing you.
- **Carrier tracking pages are confusing.** "In transit" for four days reads like nothing is happening, even when it's normal, so anxious customers reach out instead of waiting.
- **There's no proactive update.** If a package sits at a hub for two days, the customer finds out by refreshing a tracking page obsessively, not from you. By the time they email, they're already frustrated.

None of these are support problems. They're information problems that turn into support tickets because nobody closed the gap earlier.

## Fix 1: Make tracking status visible without an email

The single highest-leverage fix is a self-serve order lookup that doesn't require digging through inbox history. If a customer can type their order number or email into a page and instantly see "your order shipped Tuesday, expected Thursday," most WISMO tickets never get written.

This doesn't need to be complicated. Shopify's native order status page already does a version of this, but it's often not linked anywhere obvious, and it doesn't proactively remind anyone it exists. Put a direct link to order lookup in your footer, your post-purchase page, and your shipping confirmation email, not just buried in a "track my order" app widget nobody notices.

### A worked example

A store doing about 600 orders a month was getting roughly 180 WISMO tickets monthly, close to a third of total orders. After adding a prominent order-lookup link in the shipping confirmation email and sending one proactive "still in transit" update at the 3-day mark for anything not yet delivered, WISMO tickets dropped to around 90 a month. Same order volume, half the tickets, with no change to actual shipping speed. The orders weren't moving any faster. Customers just stopped needing to ask.

## Fix 2: Proactive updates beat reactive answers

Waiting for a customer to ask is always more expensive than telling them first. A simple rule works well for most stores: if a package hasn't moved in tracking for 48-72 hours, or delivery is later than the original estimate, send an update before they email you. It doesn't need to be an apology, just information: "Your order is still moving, here's the latest tracking update, expected delivery is now Friday."

This single habit does more to reduce ticket volume than almost anything else, because it removes the reason to write in. The customer already knows what you'd tell them.

## Fix 3: Set expectations honestly at checkout

A lot of WISMO volume is really a mismatch between what the customer expected and what actually happened. If your product page or checkout says "ships in 1-2 days" but fulfillment actually takes 4-5 days during busy weeks, you're manufacturing your own ticket volume. Being specific and slightly conservative about shipping windows (5-7 business days instead of 3-5) reduces anxious follow-ups even when actual delivery times don't change.

This connects to a broader pattern worth checking: if you're also seeing [checkout friction](/blog/reduce-abandoned-checkouts-shopify) or drop-off tied to shipping cost surprises, tightening up what you promise at checkout tends to fix both problems at once.

## Fix 4: Answer the ticket without a human reading it first

For the tickets that do come in despite the above, most WISMO questions don't need a person to read them and manually look up the order. The order number, shipping status, and carrier tracking link are all data your systems already have. A response that pulls that data automatically and replies within minutes, not hours, closes the loop faster than a human checking a queue once or twice a day, and it does it around the clock rather than during business hours only.

This is the same gap that makes [Gorgias feel more expensive than the sticker price](/blog/gorgias-vs-diy-support-cost-math) for a lot of small stores: the tool can hold the ticket, but someone still has to read and answer it. The actual cost isn't the software, it's the hours spent typing the same answer with a different order number each time.

### What this looks like at scale

If half your WISMO tickets can be answered automatically and correctly with real order data (not a canned "your order should arrive soon" non-answer), and the other half get proactive updates before they're ever written, most stores can realistically cut total WISMO volume by 40-60% within a month or two. That's not a marketing number, it's just the math of removing the two biggest reasons people write in: not knowing where the package is, and not being told before they had to ask.

## What not to do

Don't respond to WISMO tickets with generic templates that dodge the actual question ("Thanks for reaching out, your order is being processed!"). That answer often isn't even true if the order already shipped, and it guarantees a follow-up email. Every ticket answered with real, specific data closes the loop. Every ticket answered with a vague template opens a second one.

Also resist the urge to hide the order-lookup tool behind a login or account creation requirement. Most WISMO customers are checking on a gift or a one-time purchase and don't want to make an account just to see a tracking number.

## Bottom line

WISMO tickets aren't a support-team problem, they're an information-gap problem that shows up in the support queue because nothing upstream closed it first. Making tracking visible, sending proactive updates, setting honest shipping windows, and automating the actual answer for tickets that do come in can realistically cut WISMO volume in half without adding headcount or another monthly app subscription.
