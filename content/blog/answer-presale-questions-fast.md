A presale question is not just another support ticket. The shopper is still deciding whether to buy, and the useful window can close as soon as they leave the product page. A small store does not need someone watching chat all day, but it does need a reliable way to recognize buying questions, answer the common ones immediately, and give the uncertain ones a truthful next step.

The goal is not an impressive response-time number. It is to get the shopper a correct, decision-ready answer while their intent is still alive.

## Separate buying questions from ordinary support

Presale messages usually ask about one of six things:

- fit, size, dimensions, or compatibility
- delivery timing to a specific place
- what is included in the box
- stock, variants, or an expected restock
- materials, ingredients, care, or product limitations
- which of two products is right for a stated use

Tag these separately from order-status, return, and account questions. A shopper asking whether a part fits their model should not wait behind five routine tracking requests.

This does not mean ignoring existing customers. It means routing repeatable post-purchase questions toward self-service and using your limited live attention where a decision genuinely needs it. Start with the process in this guide to [reducing WISMO tickets](/blog/wismo-cut-support-tickets-in-half), then create a `presale` view or label in the inbox you already use.

## Choose a response promise you can actually keep

“We reply instantly” is a bad promise for a solo operator. Define two windows instead:

| Message timing | Honest promise |
|---|---|
| During hours you actively monitor | “We usually reply within 15 minutes.” |
| Outside those hours | “We will reply by 10:00 a.m. Eastern tomorrow.” |

Use your real schedule, not the example. A specific next time is more useful than “as soon as possible,” and it lets the customer decide whether to wait.

Shopify Inbox lets merchants set availability hours and different first replies for messages received during and outside those hours. Shopify's current [availability and automated first-reply documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/chat-settings-and-appearance/availability-and-first-reply) also recommends using that reply to request missing information and tell the customer when to expect an answer.

Turn on the notification types you can reliably notice. Shopify documents desktop, mobile, and email options in its [Inbox notification setup](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/configure-inbox/inbox-notifications/). Do not enable all of them and then train yourself to dismiss the noise. One mobile alert during staffed hours and one email fallback may be enough.

## Put the answer where the question starts

The fastest reply is the one the shopper never has to request. Pull 30 days of presale conversations and count the questions by product and type. For each repeated question, decide whether the answer belongs in:

- the product title or variant name
- the first screen of the product description
- a size or compatibility table
- the shipping estimate near the buy button
- a product-specific FAQ
- the chat greeting or an instant answer

If ten people ask whether a case fits the same device, the problem is probably not slow support. The compatibility information is hard to find or too vague. Fix that page using the [product description workflow](/blog/product-descriptions-that-rank-and-convert), then keep the saved reply as a backup.

Do not turn the product page into a wall of every question ever asked. Put purchase-critical facts high on the page and move detailed edge cases into a scannable FAQ or guide.

## Build a small answer library from verified facts

Start with five to ten replies, not fifty. Each saved reply should have three parts:

1. **The direct answer:** yes, no, a specific measurement, or a clear limitation.
2. **The reason:** the product fact that supports the answer.
3. **The next step:** the correct variant, a product link, or the information you still need.

For example:

> For a 40 cm opening, choose the 45 cm size. The usable inside width is 42 cm after the frame is assembled. Here is that variant: [product link]. If your 40 cm measurement includes trim, send a photo with the tape in place before ordering and I will check it.

Compare that with “The large should work.” The first answer gives the customer a decision and states the condition that could change it. The second transfers your uncertainty to the customer.

Shopify Inbox supports staff [quick replies](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/configure-inbox/quick-replies) that autofill saved wording before it is sent. Save the structure, but verify the product, variant, destination, and measurement every time. A canned answer should reduce typing, not bypass judgment.

## Ask for all missing information once

Slow presale conversations often fail through back-and-forth rather than a slow first response:

- “What model is it?”
- customer replies
- “Which year?”
- customer replies
- “Can you measure the opening?”

Instead, make a short checklist for each question type. A compatibility reply might request the exact model, year, relevant measurement with units, and a photo of the connection point. A delivery question needs the destination postal code, desired arrival date, and product or variant.

Keep the request limited to facts that can change the answer. Asking for an order number before the shopper has ordered only creates friction.

## Use automation for known answers, not uncertain promises

Shopify Inbox can display custom [instant answers](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/chat-settings-and-appearance/instant-answers) for common questions when the Inbox agent is not active. Shopify says the built-in Track my order option remains available regardless of whether that agent is active. That makes instant answers useful for stable policies, basic shipping ranges, and clearly documented product facts.

Keep human review for questions involving:

- safety, allergies, medical use, or regulated claims
- an exact arrival guarantee not backed by a service commitment
- compatibility that depends on an unknown model or measurement
- a large custom or wholesale order
- any exception to a published policy

Shopify's current Inbox overview says its optional Inbox agent can answer from catalog, policy, knowledge-base, and storefront content, but the feature is in early access and available only to certain merchants. Treat any AI answer as only as dependable as the source information behind it. Do not plan your workflow around a feature your store does not have.

## Send a decision, not a paragraph

A good presale reply usually fits this pattern:

> **Answer:** The medium is the better fit for the measurements you sent.<br>
> **Why:** Its inside width is 34 cm; the small is 30 cm.<br>
> **Next step:** Here is the medium in black: [link]. If you need room for a liner, size up once.

Shopify Inbox lets staff [send product links inside a conversation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/conversations). Use the exact product or variant that matches the recommendation instead of telling the shopper to search the catalog again.

Do not add a discount automatically. Most presale questions are requests for certainty, not a lower price. Answer first. If price is the real objection, handle it deliberately rather than training every person who opens chat to expect a code.

## Measure whether the workflow helps

Review the system weekly with a small scorecard:

- number of presale conversations
- median first human response during staffed hours
- percentage answered from verified saved material
- conversations that led to an order
- refunds or returns connected to a wrong presale answer
- repeated questions that should become storefront content

Conversation-to-order rate is useful only if you review the conversation quality too. A rushed recommendation that wins an order and causes a return is not a success.

Read a sample of lost conversations. If the customer disappeared after asking about sizing, was the answer late, vague, or missing a direct product link? If the answer was complete and the shopper still did not buy, do not assume speed was the problem.

## A one-hour setup for this week

1. Export or review the last 30 days of messages.
2. Tag every buying question as `presale` and group it by question type.
3. Fix the three most repeated gaps on the relevant product pages.
4. Write five verified quick replies with a direct answer, reason, and next step.
5. Set staffed hours and an exact outside-hours response promise.
6. Turn on one notification route and test it from another device.
7. Review presale conversations and resulting orders every Friday.

Once this works in one channel, connect it to the broader [conversational commerce workflow](/blog/conversational-commerce-for-small-stores). The operating rule stays simple: make common answers immediate, make uncertain answers honest, and never let the customer wait while you ask for one missing fact at a time.
