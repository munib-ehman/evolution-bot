SEO agencies love to make this sound complicated, mostly because complexity justifies the retainer. For a store doing $50k-$1M a year, the truth is simpler and less flattering to agencies: most of what actually moves the needle is unglamorous, checklist-level work you can do yourself in a weekend, not a strategy that requires a quarterly roadmap and a $2,000 monthly invoice.

Here's the checklist that actually matters, in the order it actually matters.

## 1. Fix your titles and meta descriptions first

This is the highest-leverage, lowest-effort item on the list, and most small stores skip it because it feels too basic to matter. It matters more than almost anything else you'll do.

- Every product and collection page needs a unique title tag that includes the actual term people search for, not your brand name repeated on every page.
- Meta descriptions should read like a reason to click, not a restatement of the title. "Handmade in small batches, ships within 2 days" tells a searcher something a generic description doesn't.
- Check for duplicate titles across pages using Shopify's built-in SEO fields on each product and collection. Duplicate titles confuse search engines about which page to rank for a given term.

Spend an afternoon going through your top 20 products by traffic or revenue and fixing these first. This single pass often produces more ranking movement than months of blog content.

## 2. Fix the technical basics Shopify doesn't do for you

Shopify handles a lot of SEO plumbing automatically, but not everything:

- **Broken links and 404s**: check your site for dead internal links, especially from old blog posts or collections you've since removed. Redirect them instead of leaving a dead end.
- **Image alt text**: every product image should have a short, specific alt description, not "product photo" or blank. This helps both accessibility and image search traffic, which is a real, underused source of visits for visual products.
- **Page speed**: slow pages get buried in rankings and lose customers before they convert. If you haven't checked your Core Web Vitals in the last few months, that's worth doing before adding more content, since a fast site with mediocre copy often outranks a slow site with great copy.
- **Duplicate content from variants**: if color or size variants generate separate indexed URLs with near-identical content, that can dilute rather than help your rankings. Shopify handles most of this correctly by default, but it's worth a spot check if you're using apps that generate their own variant pages.

## 3. Write product descriptions that actually contain the keywords people search

This is where most small stores leave the most value on the table. Product pages are usually the highest-intent pages on your entire site, the page someone lands on right before buying, and they're also the pages most stores write once and never revisit. Specific, real language beats vague brand copy for both search ranking and conversion. This is covered in more depth in [how to write product descriptions that rank and convert](/blog/product-descriptions-that-rank-and-convert), but the short version: answer the actual question a buyer has, in the words they'd actually search.

### A worked example

A store selling reusable water bottles had product titles like "The Aurora Bottle" with no other identifying language. After rewriting titles to "Aurora 24oz Insulated Water Bottle, Leak-Proof, Keeps Cold 24 Hours" and rewriting the description to include real search terms (leak-proof, insulated, wide mouth, dishwasher safe), organic traffic to that product page grew about 40% over two months, without a single backlink or new blog post. The product didn't change. The words describing it finally matched what people were typing into Google.

## 4. Build a small number of genuinely useful blog posts

You don't need fifty blog posts. You need a handful that answer real questions your customers already ask you, in support tickets, presale questions, and reviews. A post answering "how do I know what size to order" for a clothing store will outperform ten generic "5 tips for summer style" posts, because it matches actual search intent and actual customer need at the same time.

Pull your topic ideas directly from your support inbox. If you're getting the same presale question repeatedly, that's a signal a blog post (and probably a product page update) could intercept the question before it becomes a ticket, similar to how [proactive updates cut down WISMO tickets](/blog/wismo-cut-support-tickets-in-half) by answering the question before it's asked.

## 5. Get reviews onto product pages, not just a third-party widget

Reviews do double duty for SEO: they add fresh, unique, keyword-rich content to a page (written by customers, not you), and they build the trust signals that keep people on the page longer, which search engines read as a positive signal. Make sure reviews render as actual text on the page, not hidden behind a slow-loading widget that search engines can't fully see.

## 6. Internal linking, done simply

Link related products and blog posts to each other naturally. If someone lands on a blog post about a specific use case, link to the product that solves it. If someone's on a product page, link to a size guide or FAQ if one exists. This isn't about stuffing links everywhere, it's about making sure your best pages aren't orphaned with nothing pointing to them.

## What agencies bill for that you don't need yet

A lot of what agencies sell to small stores, elaborate keyword research decks, monthly reporting dashboards, link-building outreach campaigns, matters more once you're already doing the basics well and hitting diminishing returns. For a store under $1M in revenue, that point is usually further away than it feels. The checklist above, done thoroughly, covers the majority of the gap between where most small stores are and where they could be.

## Bottom line

SEO for a small Shopify store isn't a mystery that requires an agency retainer to solve. It's a checklist: fix your titles and meta descriptions, clean up the technical basics, write product descriptions with real language, publish a small number of genuinely useful posts, surface real reviews, and link your pages together sensibly. None of it requires a specialist, just an afternoon and some honesty about what your product pages currently say versus what people are actually searching for.
