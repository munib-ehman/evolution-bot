A Shopify preorder can validate demand or reserve units before a product is ready. It also creates a real customer obligation before you control every part of supply and fulfillment.

Use preorders only when you have a defensible quantity, a shipment window you can explain, and a delay-and-refund process ready before launch. If the supply date is still a guess, collect waitlist interest instead of money.

## Decide whether a preorder is the right tool

A preorder fits when:

- the exact product and variant are defined
- a supplier or production plan is confirmed
- you can state a reasonable shipment window
- the maximum sellable quantity is controlled
- customers benefit from reserving scarce or launch inventory
- the business can refund delayed or canceled orders

A waitlist is safer when the product, price, compliance work, production slot, or arrival date remains uncertain. A back-in-stock notification is usually clearer when the product already exists and is temporarily unavailable; the [back-in-stock guide](/blog/back-in-stock-alerts-recovering-lost-sales) covers that case.

Do not treat preorder cash as unrestricted working capital. Some or all of it may need to be refunded, while supplier, freight, and marketing bills can arrive first. Put the expected charges, cash receipts, and refund reserve into a [13-week cash flow forecast](/blog/ecommerce-13-week-cash-flow-forecast) before launching.

## Check Shopify's current requirements first

Shopify's current [preorders overview](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/pre-orders) says merchants need a preorder app and must manage the resulting purchase options in Shopify. It also says preorders are currently available only to merchants using Shopify Payments or PayPal Express, and can collect full, partial, or no payment when the order is placed.

The current [preorder setup documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/pre-orders/setup) lists important restrictions. Among them, preorders are not supported with customized checkouts, Shop Promise, or accelerated checkout methods such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay, and Google Pay. Local payment methods are also restricted, and customers cannot combine different purchase options on the same product in one checkout.

Check those pages and the selected app again before launch. Platform behavior and app capabilities can change, and an ordinary test order does not prove the preorder path works.

## Choose the payment structure deliberately

The payment model changes both cash flow and customer risk.

### Full payment now

The customer pays the entire amount at checkout. This is simple to reconcile but creates the strongest expectation and the largest potential refund liability. Keep the money visible in the cash plan until the product ships.

### Partial deposit now

The customer pays a stated deposit and the balance later. This reduces the immediate commitment but adds due-date, failed-payment, and support work. Shopify's setup guide notes that the deposit and remaining balance are shown in checkout for supported preorder arrangements.

### No payment or deferred payment

The order reserves demand and the charge happens later. This reduces upfront customer exposure but does not guarantee the later charge will succeed. Plan for expired cards, bank challenges, and customers whose circumstances change.

Choose one structure for the first pilot. The simplest model you can explain, reconcile, and support is better than a clever mix of dates and deposits.

## Cap preorder quantity from confirmed supply

The preorder cap should come from inventory you have evidence for, not the demand you hope to create.

> Sellable preorder cap = confirmed incoming units − wholesale or launch commitments − replacement and quality buffer

If 600 units are confirmed, 80 are committed to wholesale, and 30 are reserved for damage, replacements, or count variance, the maximum public preorder pool is 490 units. If 210 are already sold, 280 remain available.

This is a fictional example, not a recommended buffer. Set the buffer from supplier history, inspection results, and the cost of being short. Connect the quantity to your [demand forecast](/blog/forecast-demand-small-ecommerce-store), but do not let a forecast turn unconfirmed production into sellable inventory.

Decide when the app reserves inventory. Shopify's current [preorder management guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/pre-orders/manage-pre-orders) says reservation can happen at sale or fulfillment depending on the app. Test the setting, then reconcile preorder count, ordinary available inventory, and confirmed incoming units every day during the launch.

## Build a shipment window you can defend

Work backward from the customer promise:

> Earliest credible ship date = production completion + inspection + freight and customs + receiving and quality check + pick-and-pack time

Add a risk buffer for the parts that have actually varied in past orders. Keep supplier handoff, warehouse arrival, and customer shipment as separate milestones. “Leaves the factory in November” does not mean “ships to the customer in November.”

For US internet orders, the FTC's [Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule) says a seller needs a reasonable basis for the shipment representation. If no shipment statement is made, the guide describes a 30-day default. If the seller cannot ship on time, it explains the need to seek consent to the delay or provide a prompt refund.

That is not a substitute for legal advice, and other jurisdictions may have different requirements. The operating lesson applies broadly: put a supportable shipment promise near the buy action and have a real cancellation path.

## Put the important terms on the product page

Before the customer adds the item, show:

- that the product is a preorder
- the expected ship date or honest date range
- whether the date refers to shipment or delivery
- the amount charged now and when any balance is due
- whether preorder and ready-to-ship items ship together or separately
- how cancellation, delay consent, and refunds work
- products, destinations, or payment methods that are excluded
- a support contact

Repeat the essential information in cart, checkout, confirmation, and the order-status experience. Do not hide the date in a FAQ while the button only says “Buy now.”

Run test orders for each payment model, variant, and mixed-cart rule you intend to allow. Verify the charge, confirmation email, order tags, inventory reservation, due date, cancellation path, and refund behavior.

## Operate one preorder register

Maintain a daily register with:

- order and customer
- SKU and quantity
- amount paid and balance due
- promised shipment window
- inventory source or production batch
- current milestone
- last customer update
- next action and owner
- cancellation, consent, or refund status

Keep the register tied to the source records rather than a private spreadsheet no one reconciles. Filter unpaid and unfulfilled preorders in Shopify, then compare them with the app and incoming inventory.

Shopify's management guide explains that merchants can put fulfillment on hold, manage certain payment updates, and edit supported preorder details. It also cautions merchants to tell customers before collecting a deferred payment early, because an unexpected charge can create a dispute.

## Send updates before customers have to ask

Choose communication milestones before launch:

1. order confirmation with the original promise
2. production or inbound confirmation when it materially improves certainty
3. a pre-shipment check if a balance will be charged
4. shipping confirmation with tracking
5. a delay notice before the promised window expires

A useful update names the order, current milestone, unchanged or revised ship window, next update date, and customer options. Adapt the tone from the [support response templates](/blog/customer-support-response-templates-ecommerce), but include real preorder facts rather than a generic reassurance.

## Use a written delay playbook

The moment the promise is at risk:

1. stop or reduce new preorder sales until the available quantity and date are credible
2. identify every affected order and amount paid
3. get a revised supplier, freight, or production milestone in writing
4. send the delay notice before the original deadline
5. offer the cancellation or refund route required for the order
6. record consent and refunds
7. set the next update date and meet it

Do not silently edit the product page and assume existing customers accepted the new date. Do not replace the item with a materially different product without the customer's agreement.

## Run one narrow pilot

Start with one product, one payment model, one inventory batch, and a hard quantity cap. After fulfillment, review:

- units offered, ordered, canceled, refunded, and fulfilled
- on-time shipment rate
- failed balance payments
- support contacts per order
- contribution after app, payment, fulfillment, and refund costs
- forecast error and unused inventory
- reasons customers canceled

A preorder is successful when it improves a buying or planning decision and the store keeps its promise. Start by writing the cap, ship window, payment terms, and delay message. If any one of those is still blank, the preorder is not ready to open.
