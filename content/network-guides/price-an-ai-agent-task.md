There is no useful universal price for an AI agent. A one-off comparison of public pages and a daily workflow inside a private account have different access needs, failure risks and delivery expectations. Begin by defining the work, then choose a budget you can discuss with a provider.

New requests on Evolution show the buyer’s monthly budget in USD. Builders can use that budget and the requested frequency to assess whether the work is viable. They are not an independent platform valuation or a promise that every request will attract a builder.

## Define the unit you are buying

Avoid paying for a label like “research agent” without defining the research. A useful unit has a measurable boundary: a fixed number of records, a known source set, a specific output format, and a delivery condition.

For example, a pilot could compare a small set of supplier documents and return a table with source references. The volume, source quality and required accuracy matter more than the word “agent.” If you change those inputs substantially, expect to discuss scope again.

## Separate task price from ongoing costs

Ask whether the quoted amount covers a single run, repeated use, setup, hosting, third-party tools or access to the provider's service. Do not assume a one-time checkout includes unlimited future runs.

If a workflow calls paid APIs or depends on another subscription, clarify who pays and what happens when a spending limit is reached. Do not authorize an open-ended bill because the initial demonstration looked inexpensive. Keep the first pilot within a budget you are comfortable losing if the result proves unsuitable.

## Agree on acceptance before delivery

Write down what you will inspect. Does each row need a source link? Are duplicates unacceptable? Is an empty value better than a guess? Does the output need to import into a particular application?

Distinguish a broken result from a changed request. A missing required field might fail the original brief; adding a new data source after delivery is a scope change. Discuss revision and refund terms with the provider rather than assuming the platform defines them for every service.

## Keep the first purchase small

A limited pilot lets both sides discover practical problems without committing to a large workflow. Use non-sensitive sample data where possible and withhold broad permissions until the task has earned them. A higher budget does not make unrestricted account access safer.

Buyers and builders agree directly on payment, setup and ongoing maintenance. Ask what the monthly price includes and who fixes the workflow if a data source changes.

Use the [request-writing template](/guides/write-an-ai-agent-request) to connect the amount to a concrete deliverable. A good budget conversation is not about finding the cheapest model. It is about making the work clear enough that you and the builder know what is being bought.
