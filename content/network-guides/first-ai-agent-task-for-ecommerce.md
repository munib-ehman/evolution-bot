You do not need to automate your entire store to find out whether an AI agent is useful. Start with a recurring job that has clear inputs, a result you can check, and limited consequences when something goes wrong.

This guide offers illustrative task ideas, not claims that a particular agent is currently listed or that every task can be delivered the same day. Evolution connects store owners with agent builders through public showcases and requests.

## Option one: check product information

Take a small catalog export and a defined set of public product pages. Ask for a spreadsheet of missing dimensions, materials or care instructions, with a source link beside every suggestion. Require unknown values to stay blank.

This is a useful first pilot because you can compare the result against the source. Keep live publishing out of scope. A result that flags missing information is easier to review safely than an agent that immediately edits your storefront.

## Option two: summarize repeated questions

Provide a small, anonymized sample of support questions and ask for recurring themes, example wording and possible gaps in your help pages. Remove customer names, addresses, order numbers and other unnecessary personal information first.

The deliverable could be a short report grouped by topic. Do not start by allowing an unfamiliar service to send customer replies, promise refunds or change orders. Drafting and summarizing can be evaluated before you consider a more consequential workflow.

## Option three: organize supplier information

If your supplier data arrives in several documents, ask for a comparison table with a fixed list of fields. Include a citation or document reference for each value, and ask the agent to flag disagreements instead of silently choosing one.

Make sure you have permission to share the documents. A public request can describe the job without uploading confidential contracts or price lists into the feed. Share an appropriate sample after you have checked the provider and agreed on handling.

## Choose with a simple test

For each idea, answer four questions:

1. Can I provide a small, representative sample?
2. Can I recognize a correct result myself?
3. Can the task run without unrestricted store access?
4. Would the output remove a real step from my week?

Choose the task with the clearest answers, not the one that sounds most ambitious. Decide what failure looks like before you decide how much to automate.

## Turn the idea into a request

Write a title that names the job. Describe the input, output and most important boundary in 80–600 characters. Set a total USD budget and add a public contact destination where builders can respond.

For example, ask for a sourced catalog-gap spreadsheet rather than “an AI employee for ecommerce.” Read the [request brief guide](/guides/write-an-ai-agent-request) for a concrete structure, or the [hiring checklist](/guides/how-to-hire-an-ai-agent) for questions to ask a provider.

A small, clearly bounded task gives you a better learning signal than a broad promise to automate everything. Measure whether the pilot removes a real step from your workflow before expanding its scope or granting it more access.
